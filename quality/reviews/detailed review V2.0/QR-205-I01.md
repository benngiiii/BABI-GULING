## 3. Review Findings and Suggested Improvements

### QR-205-I01 — Minor Findings

**Finding 1: Clarify the Relationship Between Personal Data and PII**

Section 2.1 describes personal data as also being called personally identifiable information (PII).

These terms are related, but they are not always interchangeable. Their definitions and legal usage can differ between jurisdictions.

**Suggested improvement:**

Explain that personal data is the terminology used in frameworks such as the GDPR, while PII is commonly used in other privacy contexts. Avoid presenting them as universally identical terms.

**Finding 2: Improve the Explanation of Anonymisation**

The chapter correctly explains that anonymised data may sometimes be re-identified. However, learners could benefit from a clearer distinction between removing direct identifiers and achieving effective anonymisation.

**Suggested improvement:**

Add a short example showing that removing a name or student ID does not necessarily prevent identification when other details can be combined.

**Finding 3: Clarify the Four-Fifths Rule's Scope**

The explanation of the four-fifths rule is generally correct and the calculations are consistent.

However, learners should understand that this is a screening indicator associated with US employment-selection guidance, not a universal legal definition of fairness.

**Suggested improvement:**

Emphasise that the rule is a preliminary screening measure. Its interpretation depends on the legal and practical context, and additional statistical and contextual analysis may be necessary.

**Finding 4: Improve the Distinction Between Bias and Hallucination**

Section 5.2 compares bias and hallucination effectively. However, the chapter could reinforce this distinction with a simple comparison table.

**Suggested improvement:**

| Concept       | Main Concern                                       | Example                                           |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Bias          | Unequal or systematically less favourable outcomes | A hiring system rejects one group more frequently |
| Hallucination | False, fabricated, or unsupported information      | A chatbot invents a research paper                |

This would help beginners remember that a system can produce factually correct information while still producing unfair outcomes.

**Finding 5: Add a More Detailed Study-Time Breakdown**

The estimated study time is approximately 3–4 hours. However, the chapter does not provide a complete time allocation for all five topics and their activities.

**Suggested improvement:**

Add an approximate study-time breakdown for each topic, including reading, worked examples, activities, and discussion. Ensure the total is consistent with the stated 3–4 hours.

**Finding 6: Verify the Bias Diagram Asset**

The chapter references:

`assets/bias-occurence-diagram.png`

The image itself was not included in the supplied text.

**Suggested improvement:**

Confirm that the image exists in the correct repository location and that the Markdown path works. Also check the spelling of `occurence` in the filename and consider correcting it to `occurrence` if the asset is being renamed.

**Finding 7: Strengthen the Final Reflection Assessment Criteria**

The final reflection asks learners to write approximately 150–200 words and lists the expected content. This is useful, but the assessment criteria could be more explicit.

**Suggested improvement:**

Add a short assessment checklist covering:

* Identification of responsible people or organisations.
* Application of at least two responsible AI principles.
* Explanation of human oversight.
* A practical prevention or improvement measure.

This would make the expected standard easier for both learners and instructors to evaluate.

**Finding 8: Clarify the Meaning of Human Responsibility**

The statement that AI systems do not have human intentions or moral responsibility provides a useful starting point.

However, the chapter could distinguish between the AI system's role and the legal or professional responsibilities of the people and organisations involved.

**Suggested improvement:**

Clarify that responsibility depends on the roles, decisions, and applicable duties of developers, deployers, organisations, and users. Avoid suggesting that every person involved automatically carries the same responsibility.

**Finding 9: Verify References and External Links**

The chapter includes relevant academic papers, institutional frameworks, regulations, and legal cases.

However, the original source documents and external links were not independently checked during this review.

**Suggested improvement:**

Before submission, verify:

* Author names and publication years.
* Article titles and publication details.
* DOI and URL accuracy.
* Whether each source supports the statement for which it is cited.
* Whether legal information is presented in the correct jurisdiction and context.

The intentionally fabricated Henderson & Zhou (2019) citation is clearly identified as a teaching example and should remain excluded from the actual reference list.

**Finding 10: Complete Independent Learner Testing**

The chapter contains many activities and answer keys, but no evidence was supplied showing that a learner who did not write the chapter followed it independently.

**Suggested improvement:**

Ask another team member to study selected sections without additional explanations from the author. Record:

* What the learner understood.
* Which terms or explanations caused confusion.
* Whether the worked examples were easy to follow.
* What revisions were made after the test.

**Finding 11: Complete Independent Cross-Review**

The supplied chapter identifies Brian Djoni Suzanna as its author. Since Brian is also the Quality Lead, this review should be treated as a preliminary quality assessment rather than the required independent cross-review.

**Suggested improvement:**

Assign another team member to review Chapter 5 independently and document their findings and review result.

## 5. Positive Findings

1. **Strong chapter structure:** The chapter progresses logically from AI limitations to practical responsibilities and real-world discussion.

2. **Good connection with previous chapters:** The opening recap connects AI applications from Chapter 4 with the ethical concerns discussed in Chapter 5.

3. **Clear learning outcomes:** The outcomes use measurable actions such as explain, identify, calculate, recognise, and apply.

4. **Practical explanation of bias:** The life-cycle table helps learners understand that bias can enter during data collection, labelling, training, testing, and deployment.

5. **Useful mathematical application:** The selection-rate and impact-ratio examples provide a practical way to introduce fairness screening.

6. **Good privacy scenario:** The clinic-letter example connects privacy concepts with a realistic situation involving sensitive information.

7. **Practical hallucination verification routine:** The five-step process gives learners a useful method for checking AI-generated claims and citations.

8. **Responsible AI checklist:** The checklist translates abstract principles into questions that learners can apply to real situations.

9. **Relevant real-world cases:** Gender Shades, healthcare risk prediction, Amazon's recruiting tool, and Mata v. Avianca help demonstrate why these issues matter.

10. **Comprehensive practice materials:** The chapter includes True/False, Multiple Choice, and Open-Ended questions with answers and explanations.

11. **Good distinction between concepts:** The chapter correctly explains that bias and hallucination are different problems and that a system can experience either or both.

12. **Appropriate beginner level:** Programming is optional, technical terms are explained, and learners can complete the main activities without programming knowledge.

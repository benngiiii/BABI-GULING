## 3. Review Findings and Suggested Improvements

### QR-204-I01 — Findings and Suggested Improvements

**Finding 1: The distinction between AI systems and ordinary automation should be more consistent across application areas.**

The chapter correctly explains that the adaptive quiz example in Education uses fixed rules and is not necessarily a trained machine-learning model.

It also correctly identifies the moving-average trading example in Finance as rule-based automation rather than necessarily an AI system.

However, some examples in other application areas could create the impression that every automated digital service is automatically an AI application.

For example, multiple-choice marking and ordinary route calculation can be performed using conventional programmed rules without machine learning.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Add a short clarification explaining that:

* Some applications use machine-learning models.
* Some applications use fixed rules or conventional automation.
* Some real-world systems combine AI models with ordinary software and automation.

Where appropriate, clarify which part of an application actually uses AI.

This will strengthen consistency with the distinction between AI and traditional programming introduced in earlier chapters.

**Finding 2: The Education section should distinguish AI assessment from ordinary automated marking.**

Section 2.2 includes automated assessment support and multiple-choice marking as an example.

However, multiple-choice marking can be performed using simple answer-matching rules and does not necessarily require AI.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Clarify that ordinary multiple-choice marking is rule-based automation. AI may provide additional assessment support through tasks such as analysing written responses, identifying patterns in student performance, or generating feedback.

This will prevent learners from confusing automation with machine learning.

**Finding 3: The Transportation section should clarify that not every navigation or route-planning function necessarily uses AI.**

Navigation systems are listed as AI applications involving prediction and route planning.

However, route planning can also rely on conventional algorithms and optimization methods.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Explain that some navigation services use AI or machine-learning models to estimate traffic conditions and arrival times, while other functions may use conventional algorithms.

This distinction will make the explanation more technically precise.

**Finding 4: The final learning activity needs a clearer assessment framework.**

The final activity asks learners to select one application area and analyse its purpose, data, AI task, output, benefits, limitations, consequences, and need for human oversight.

The activity is relevant and encourages independent thinking.

However, only one sample answer is provided, and there is no explicit assessment rubric describing what makes a complete answer.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Add a short assessment guide or expected-answer checklist.

For example:

| Assessment Area        | Expected Answer                                                      |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Problem Identification | Clearly identifies a real-world problem.                             |
| Data Requirements      | Identifies relevant information needed by the system.                |
| AI Task                | Correctly identifies the main AI task.                               |
| System Output          | Explains what the system produces.                                   |
| Benefits               | Identifies a realistic advantage.                                    |
| Limitations            | Explains at least one possible weakness.                             |
| Human Oversight        | Provides a reasonable explanation of whether human review is needed. |

This will make the activity easier for learners to complete and easier for teachers to assess.

**Finding 5: The healthcare performance examples could include a clearer explanation of how the decision threshold affects results.**

The healthcare section explains sensitivity, specificity, precision, and accuracy using two numerical examples.

It also explains why disease prevalence can influence precision.

This is a strong part of the chapter.

However, the relationship between the decision threshold and the balance between false positives and false negatives is not developed in detail.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Add a short beginner-level explanation that changing a classification threshold may change the number of positive and negative predictions.

Explain that a lower threshold may identify more possible positive cases but can also increase false positives, while a higher threshold may reduce false positives but miss more actual cases.

Keep the explanation conceptual rather than introducing unnecessary mathematical complexity.

**Finding 6: The SAE driving automation explanation could be made more precise.**

The chapter provides a useful table explaining Levels 0–5.

However, the Level 5 description uses the wording "under all conditions covered by the framework," which may leave beginners uncertain about how Level 5 differs from Level 4.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Clarify that Level 4 automation is limited to its defined operating conditions, while Level 5 is intended to perform the driving task under all roadway and environmental conditions that a human driver could reasonably manage.

Keep the explanation consistent with the terminology of SAE J3016.

**Finding 7: The financial trading example should maintain clear separation between educational calculation and real-world trading performance.**

The moving-average example correctly demonstrates a fixed-rule system and shows that following a rule does not guarantee a positive result.

The assumptions and calculation steps are explained.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Maintain the existing educational disclaimer and consider adding one short explanation that the example excludes real-world factors such as transaction costs, market liquidity, and execution differences.

This will reinforce that the example demonstrates mathematical rules rather than a reliable financial strategy.

**Finding 8: The references and external learning resources require final verification.**

The chapter includes academic research and institutional references related to healthcare, education, recommendation systems, financial trading, and transportation.

These references support the chapter's academic direction.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Before final submission, verify:

* Accuracy of author names and publication years.
* Correct article titles and publication details.
* DOI and external link accessibility.
* Whether each reference supports the related explanation.
* Consistency of reference formatting.

The references have not been independently checked against their original publications during this review.

**Finding 9: Independent learner testing evidence is not included in the supplied material.**

The chapter contains multiple activities and practice questions, but the supplied file does not include evidence that a learner other than the author has followed the chapter.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Ask a teammate who did not write Chapter 4 to study the material and complete selected activities.

Record:

* Which concepts the learner understood.
* Which explanations were confusing.
* Whether the numerical examples were easy to follow.
* Whether the activities were manageable.
* What changes were made after receiving feedback.

**Finding 10: Independent cross-review is still required because the author is also the Quality Lead.**

The supplied chapter identifies Brian Djoni Suzanna as its author.

Since Brian is also responsible for project quality assurance, this preliminary review cannot replace the independent peer-review requirement.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Assign a different team member to review Chapter 4.

The independent reviewer should check the technical explanations, examples, calculations, learning activities, references, and suggested revisions.

Record the reviewer's actual name, feedback, and recheck evidence in the review documentation.

## 5. Positive Findings

The review identifies several important strengths.

1. **Comprehensive Application Coverage:** The chapter covers five major sectors and demonstrates how AI is used in different real-world environments.

2. **Clear Learning Objectives:** The learning outcomes cover identification, explanation, interpretation, comparison, and evaluation of AI applications.

3. **Strong Healthcare Example:** The confusion matrix and performance calculations provide a useful mathematical application of AI evaluation.

4. **Good Numerical Demonstrations:** The healthcare and financial examples allow learners to follow calculations step by step.

5. **Practical Education Example:** The adaptive quiz demonstrates how a system can adjust learning difficulty based on student performance.

6. **Clear Transportation Workflow:** The Sense–Perceive–Predict–Plan–Act sequence provides an understandable explanation of how autonomous driving systems process information.

7. **Relevant Everyday Applications:** Recommendation systems and spam filtering help learners connect AI concepts with familiar digital services.

8. **Useful Finance Discussion:** The chapter explains fraud detection, anomaly detection, algorithmic trading, backtesting, and overfitting while highlighting their limitations.

9. **Good Visual Support:** Mermaid flowcharts and comparison tables help explain processes and relationships.

10. **Consistent Attention to Limitations:** Each major application area discusses possible weaknesses and the importance of human oversight.

11. **Meaningful Learning Activities:** Activities are provided throughout the chapter rather than only at the end.

12. **Strong Cross-Topic Comparison:** The comparison table helps learners understand why different AI applications require different evaluation standards.

13. **Useful Chapter Summary:** The key takeaways reinforce the major concepts and distinguish AI systems from ordinary rule-based automation.

Overall, Chapter 4 demonstrates a strong understanding of how AI concepts can be connected to practical applications.

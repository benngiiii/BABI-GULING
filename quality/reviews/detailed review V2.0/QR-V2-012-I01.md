## 3. Review Findings and Suggested Improvements

### QR-V2-012-I01 — Positive Review Findings

**Finding 1: Complete team member information**

Section 1 identifies all five team members, including their Chinese names, student IDs and assigned roles.

This provides a clear reference for identifying project members and their responsibilities.

**Finding 2: Strengths are documented**

Section 2 records the individual strengths of each member.

This helps the team understand its available skills and distribute work according to individual capabilities.

**Finding 3: Responsibilities are clearly assigned**

Section 3 describes each member's main responsibilities based on their project role.

The assignments cover visual design, quality review, video production, content coordination and team management.

**Finding 4: Deliverable ownership is established**

Section 4 provides a deliverable ownership table with separate assignments for preparation, review and compilation.

This makes responsibility for each major project output easier to identify.

**Finding 5: Status labels are clearly defined**

Section 5 explains the seven status labels:

- Not Started
- In Progress
- Draft Ready
- Completed
- Needs Revision
- Blocked
- To confirm

The distinction between a draft and a completed, evidenced task is particularly useful for tracking progress.

**Finding 6: Stage 1 tasks are documented**

Section 6.1 contains 23 Stage 1 tasks covering project planning, chapter development, reviews, repository preparation, documentation and submission.

The completed status records provide a useful history of the team's first project stage.

**Finding 7: Stage 2 task coverage is comprehensive**

Section 6.2 divides Stage 2 into six work areas:

- Chapters
- Project Documents
- Learning Resources
- Quality and Review
- Logs and Evidence
- Compile and Submit

The tasks cover the main activities required to prepare the Stage 2 deliverables.

**Finding 8: Stage 3 tasks are planned**

Section 6.3 provides 14 tasks for the final project stage.

These include live teaching, feedback collection, content corrections, final resource preparation, quality review, documentation and submission.

**Finding 9: Ongoing responsibilities are included**

Section 6.4 identifies recurring responsibilities for task updates, meeting records, peer review and AI-use documentation.

This supports continuous project management rather than relying only on deadline-based tasks.

**Finding 10: Task maintenance rules are understandable**

Section 7 explains who updates task statuses and what evidence is needed before a task can be marked Completed.

It also establishes that unverified status labels should be replaced by confirmed values.

**Finding 11: Project purpose and learning outcomes are connected**

Section 8 describes the project's educational purpose and the three connected learning resources.

Its six learning outcomes cover AI fundamentals, classification, workflow, AI approaches, applications and ethical concerns.

**Finding 12: Workflow and schedule are presented visually**

Section 9 includes workflow and schedule figures, together with a table describing the team's working arrangements.

The combination of visual and written information helps communicate the overall development process.

**Finding 13: Review and compilation responsibilities are identifiable**

The task tables assign owners, reviewers, deadlines and evidence locations for the main activities.

This supports accountability and makes it easier to follow up on unfinished work.

**Finding 14: Evidence is included in task tracking**

The Evidence columns provide locations or records associated with individual tasks.

This reinforces the principle that task completion should be supported by an actual output.

**Finding 15: Document organisation is logical**

The document follows a clear structure, beginning with team information and responsibilities before moving into task tracking, project purpose, workflow and revision history.

The use of separate subsections for Stage 1, Stage 2 and Stage 3 makes the task list easier to navigate.

**Finding 16: Revision history records the V2.0 update**

The revision history identifies the transition from the Stage 1 task list to V2.0.

It records the expansion to eight chapters, the addition of Stage 1–3 tasks, and the inclusion of logs, evidence and previous-year project review activities.

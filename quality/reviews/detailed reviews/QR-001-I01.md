## 3. Review Findings and Suggested Improvements

**Overall Finding:**

Chapter 1 provides a clear and beginner-friendly introduction to Artificial Intelligence. It explains what AI is, introduces important historical developments, compares traditional programming with machine learning, and defines the terminology needed for the rest of the module.

The worked example of a spam filter is a particular strength because it demonstrates how training data can be used to construct a simple model and how that model can make predictions on new examples. The practice questions also give learners an opportunity to apply what they have learned.

However, several improvements are recommended before the chapter is considered ready for final submission. These mainly concern consistency between the learning outcomes and chapter content, verification of references and image attribution, and final Markdown checks.

### QR-001-I01 — Content Clarification, Reference Verification, and Formatting Improvements

**Severity:** Medium
**Status:** Open

The following points are recommended for improvement as part of this initial review.

**1. Align the stated historical learning outcome with the timeline (Section 1.2)**

The Learning Outcomes section states that students should be able to describe five key moments in the history of AI. However, the historical timeline includes nine entries, ranging from Turing's 1950 paper to the public release of ChatGPT in 2022.

The additional milestones are useful, but the difference between the stated learning outcome and the actual timeline could create unnecessary confusion.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Either revise the learning outcome to say that students should be able to describe several important moments in AI history, or identify five milestones that learners are specifically expected to remember.

The full timeline can remain as additional context.

**2. Verify the historical claims and references (Sections 1.2 and 1.7)**

The chapter includes important historical claims about the Turing Test, the Dartmouth workshop, Arthur Samuel's checkers program, Deep Blue, AlexNet, AlphaGo, Transformers, and ChatGPT.

The reference list contains relevant academic publications and other sources. However, the chapter text should be checked against these references to ensure that the dates, descriptions, and historical interpretations are supported.

Particular attention should be given to the distinction between the 1955 Dartmouth proposal and the workshop held in 1956, as well as the descriptions of what made each milestone significant.

**Suggested Improvement:**

1. Verify the historical dates and descriptions against the listed references.
2. Ensure that each reference supports the claim for which it is used.
3. Add brief inline citations where they would help readers identify the source of a specific historical claim.
4. Keep the explanations concise and suitable for beginners.

The existing timeline does not need to be rewritten if the information is confirmed.

**3. Confirm image sources and licensing information (Section 1.2)**

The chapter includes images of Alan Turing, a computer similar to Deep Blue, and several diagrams.

The captions for the Turing and Deep Blue images explicitly state that the licensing information still needs to be confirmed before submission. This should be addressed before the chapter is distributed as a final learning resource.

The team-created diagrams also need to be available at their referenced repository paths.

**Suggested Improvement:**

1. Check the original file page for each externally sourced image.
2. Confirm the image's license and any required attribution.
3. Record the correct source, creator, and license information in the caption or an appropriate attribution section.
4. Confirm that the team-created figures are stored at the specified paths and display correctly.
5. Replace any image that cannot be used under the project's requirements.

Do not mark the image attributions as verified until these checks have actually been completed.

**4. Verify the Markdown rendering and navigation**

The chapter uses a Table of Contents, nested headings, Markdown tables, and embedded images. These elements appear reasonably organized in the supplied text, but the final presentation depends on the actual Markdown renderer and the availability of the referenced assets.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Preview the chapter in GitHub and check that:

* All Table of Contents links navigate to the correct headings.
* All referenced images load correctly.
* Tables remain readable and properly aligned.
* Figure captions appear directly below the corresponding images.
* The chapter headings and numbering are consistent throughout the document.

These checks are intended to prevent presentation problems rather than require major formatting changes.

**5. Preserve the accuracy and limitations of the spam-filter example (Section 1.5)**

The spam-filter example is clearly explained and demonstrates the relationship between labelled data, training, a model, and inference.

The scoring calculations in the supplied examples are consistent with the stated scoring table and decision rule. The chapter also appropriately explains that the method is simplified and that its predictions can be incorrect.

One point worth emphasizing is that the scores are derived from a very small set of example emails. They illustrate the basic idea of learning from examples, rather than demonstrating a reliable real-world spam detection system.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Retain the worked example and its existing limitations. Ensure that the final presentation continues to distinguish this manually constructed teaching model from more advanced statistical or neural-network-based spam filters.

No major rewrite is necessary.

**6. Check the consistency of the learning outcomes, terminology, and reuse plan**

The chapter introduces eight core AI terms and includes additional terminology such as labels. The worked example and practice questions reinforce several of these terms.

The reuse plan also identifies which sections and visuals are intended for the Study Guide, PPT Slides, and Video Tutorial.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Check that the final learning materials use the same terminology and that the planned resources preserve the chapter's intended learning objectives. In particular, the explanations of training, inference, and models should remain consistent across the written material, slides, and video.

This is a consistency check rather than a request to add substantial new content.

**Acceptance Criteria:**

* The historical learning outcome is consistent with the intended use of the timeline.
* Important historical claims and references have been checked.
* Image attribution and licensing information is confirmed before final distribution.
* The Markdown document and referenced images display correctly in the intended viewing environment.
* The spam-filter example retains its correct calculations and explanation of limitations.
* The terminology and planned learning resources remain consistent with the chapter's learning objectives.

## 5. Positive Findings

The following strengths were identified during the initial review:

1. **Clear introduction to AI.** The opening section defines AI in accessible language and acknowledges that different sources may define the field differently.

2. **Relevant everyday examples.** The examples involving face recognition, video recommendations, text suggestions, translation, spam filtering, and chatbots help connect AI concepts to familiar experiences.

3. **Useful historical overview.** The timeline introduces major developments in AI research and explains why they matter, rather than simply listing dates.

4. **Clear comparison of programming approaches.** The chapter distinguishes traditional rule-based programming from machine learning and appropriately notes that not all AI systems learn from data.

5. **Consistent terminology explanations.** The terminology table gives beginners a useful reference for understanding key concepts throughout the module.

6. **Effective worked example.** The spam-filter example explains the complete process from labelled data to training, model construction, and inference.

7. **Appropriate discussion of limitations.** The chapter explains how a small training dataset can influence a model's predictions and why a model may fail on unfamiliar examples.

8. **Useful practice questions.** The eleven questions include both conceptual questions and calculations using the spam-filter scoring model.

9. **Explained answers.** The answer section provides explanations that help learners understand the reasoning behind each answer.

10. **Practical reuse plan.** The chapter identifies how the written material, diagrams, and worked example can be adapted for the team's other learning resources.

Overall, Chapter 1 provides a strong foundation for readers who have no previous experience with AI or programming.

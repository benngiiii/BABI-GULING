## 3. Review Findings and Suggested Improvements

**Overall Finding:**

The Master Task List provides a clear overview of the team's structure, learning objectives, planned resources, and general development process.

The document establishes a useful foundation for coordinating the AI Overview project. The team members and their responsibilities are identified, and the Rough Plan explains how the team intends to organize its work across the project stages.

However, the document currently functions more as a general project plan than a detailed task-tracking document.

Several related improvements are recommended to make the Master Task List more useful for monitoring Stage 1 progress and coordinating individual responsibilities.

### QR-006-I01 — Task Tracking, Responsibility, and Progress Management Improvements

**Severity:** Medium
**Status:** Open

The following points are recommended for improvement:

**1. Add a specific and trackable Master Task List**

The document identifies the general responsibilities of each team member but does not include a detailed list of individual project tasks.

For example, the Quality Lead is responsible for checking submitted materials and determining whether revisions are necessary.

However, the document does not identify individual review tasks, their expected outputs, deadlines, or current statuses.

Similarly, the responsibilities of the Visual Design Lead, Video Lead, CIO and Content Lead, and CEO are described broadly without a corresponding task-tracking table.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Add a dedicated section titled **Master Task List**.

The section should identify individual tasks and include the following information:

* Task ID.
* Task description.
* Responsible member.
* Expected deliverable.
* Deadline.
* Current status.
* Supporting evidence or relevant file, where available.

This would allow the team to monitor specific work rather than relying only on general role descriptions.

The existing Responsibilities Based on Roles section should be retained because it provides a useful overview of the team's organizational structure.

**2. Clearly identify the current progress of individual tasks**

The Rough Plan explains the team's intended workflow, including outlining, drafting, self-checking, reviewing, and revising materials.

However, the document does not clearly distinguish completed tasks from ongoing or planned activities.

For example, the Markdown development plan describes the process of preparing an outline, writing a sample section, and developing full drafts.

It does not indicate which of these activities have already been completed during Stage 1.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Introduce consistent progress labels for individual tasks.

Recommended labels include:

* **Not Started:** The task has been assigned but work has not begun.
* **In Progress:** Work has started but is not yet complete.
* **Completed:** The assigned task has been completed.
* **Needs Revision:** The task output requires corrections or improvements.
* **Blocked:** The task cannot continue until another issue is resolved.

These labels would make the Master Task List more useful for tracking development and identifying unfinished work.

The statuses should reflect actual progress and should not be assigned without confirmation from the responsible members.

**3. Clarify ownership of specific deliverables**

The Responsibilities Based on Roles section identifies each member's general duties.

However, some descriptions combine several different types of work.

For example, the Visual Design Lead is assigned responsibilities involving visual design, Word documents, study modules, and PowerPoint slides.

Although this provides a general description of the role, it does not identify the specific deliverables assigned to that member.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Keep the existing role descriptions but clarify responsibility for individual deliverables in the Master Task List.

For each task, identify the member responsible for completing the work.

Where necessary, distinguish between the member preparing the material and the member reviewing or compiling it.

This would reduce uncertainty and help the team coordinate work more effectively.

**4. Add task-level deadlines for Stage 1**

The Rough Plan provides internal and official deadlines for Stage 1, Stage 2, and Stage 3.

This is useful for understanding the overall project schedule.

However, the document does not specify when individual tasks should be completed.

For example, separate deadlines are not identified for drafting materials, conducting quality reviews, completing revisions, and preparing the final Stage 1 submission.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Add deadlines for major individual tasks in the Master Task List.

The team should prioritize the remaining Stage 1 work and ensure that review and revision activities can be completed before the official deadline.

Task-level deadlines should be agreed upon by the responsible members and should reflect the team's actual schedule.

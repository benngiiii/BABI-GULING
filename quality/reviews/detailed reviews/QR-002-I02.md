## 3. Review Findings and Suggested Improvements

### QR-002-I01 — Reference Verification and Claim Support

* **Severity:** Low
* **Status:** Open
* **Category:** References and Citations

**Finding:**

The chapter includes references from IBM, Stanford HAI, and NIST. These are useful starting points for readers who want to learn more about AI.

However, the chapter covers several distinct topics, including the four functionality categories, Machine Learning, Generative AI, and claims about the current status of AGI and self-aware AI. The reference list should be checked to ensure that the selected sources directly support the claims attributed to them.

The chapter text also does not include inline citations that clearly connect particular claims to individual references.

**Suggested Improvement:**

1. Verify that each listed source is accessible and relevant to the topic for which it is used.
2. Add inline citations for claims that benefit from direct attribution, particularly definitions and statements about the current status of AI capabilities.
3. Consider adding a source that specifically discusses the functionality-based classification framework if the existing references do not cover it adequately.
4. Keep the references section concise and appropriate for an introductory chapter.

**Acceptance Criteria:**

* The listed references are accessible and relevant.
* Important claims can be traced to suitable sources.
* No unsupported attribution is introduced during revision.

### QR-002-I02 — Classification Diagram Rendering

* **Severity:** Low
* **Status:** Pending Verification
* **Category:** Markdown Formatting

**Finding:**

Section 2.5 uses a plain-text diagram to summarize the relationship between capability, functionality, Machine Learning, and Generative AI.

The accompanying explanation correctly notes that the diagram is simplified and that the categories are not mutually exclusive. However, the alignment of a text-based diagram may vary across Markdown viewers or exported formats.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Preview the diagram in the project's actual Markdown viewer and final export. If the alignment is difficult to follow, consider using a Markdown-compatible diagram format or a simple image.

The diagram should also preserve the important distinction that Machine Learning and Generative AI describe different aspects of AI from the capability framework.

**Acceptance Criteria:**

* The diagram is readable in the intended viewing format.
* The relationships between the frameworks remain clear.
* The diagram does not imply that all categories are mutually exclusive or belong to one standardized taxonomy.

### QR-002-I03 — Technical Wording and Framework Limitations

* **Severity:** Low
* **Status:** Open
* **Category:** Content Accuracy and Terminology

**Finding:**

The chapter appropriately explains that the functionality-based framework is a commonly taught model rather than a universally standardized scientific taxonomy. It also distinguishes between producing emotional language and demonstrating consciousness.

A few descriptions could still be interpreted too broadly if readers overlook these qualifications. For example, the limited-memory category includes systems that use stored historical information, while reactive machines are described in terms of how they use past information. Actual systems may have more complex architectures than these introductory categories suggest.

The existing caveats already address much of this concern, so a major rewrite is unnecessary.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Retain the current explanations and ensure that the qualifications remain visible when the chapter is formatted. Where useful, emphasize that these categories are simplified teaching concepts and that a real system's classification depends on its design and the definition being applied.

The explanation of Deep Blue should also remain focused on its role as a classic example of reactive AI rather than implying that all chess-playing systems necessarily belong to the same category.

**Acceptance Criteria:**

* The chapter continues to distinguish simplified teaching frameworks from universally accepted technical taxonomies.
* Examples are presented as illustrations rather than universal rules.
* Existing caveats about hypothetical capabilities and current AI systems are preserved.

## 3. Review Findings and Suggested Improvements

### Finding 1 — Inaccurate Chapter 2 Reference

**Location:** Section 3.4 — Algorithms and Models
**Priority:** Low
**Status:** Open

**Previous Finding:**

QR-003-I01 identified a mismatch between Chapter 3's description of Chapter 2 and the actual focus of Chapter 2.

**Recheck Result:**

The available chapter text contains the following statement:

> Chapter 2 covers supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning.

However, the Chapter 2 material reviewed previously focuses mainly on capability-based and functionality-based classifications of AI. The statement therefore does not accurately describe the preceding chapter.

Although the distinction between classification and regression as supervised learning tasks is relevant, the cross-chapter reference should not attribute topics to Chapter 2 that it does not adequately cover.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Replace the current parenthetical statement with:

> Classification and regression are both supervised learning tasks. Other approaches include unsupervised learning and reinforcement learning.

This keeps the explanation relevant without making an inaccurate claim about Chapter 2.

**Expected Result:** Section 3.4 explains the relationship between these learning approaches without an incorrect cross-chapter reference.

---

### Finding 2 — Training-Based and Inference-Time Improvements

**Location:** Section 3.8 — Training vs. Inference
**Priority:** Medium
**Status:** Open

**Previous Finding:**

QR-003-I01 identified that the section titled *Improving results without retraining* included both inference-time techniques and methods that require training.

**Recheck Result:**

The available chapter text lists the following techniques under the heading *Improving results without retraining*:

* Prompting
* Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
* Tools and agents
* Fine-tuning
* Training from scratch

Prompting, RAG, and tools can improve results without changing the model's learned parameters through retraining.

Fine-tuning, however, involves further training an existing model. Training from scratch involves training a new model. These methods do not fit the current heading.

The issue is not that the methods are irrelevant to AI improvement. It is that they are grouped under a heading that incorrectly suggests none of them involves training.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Retain the existing explanation but separate the methods into two categories.

**Inference-Time Improvements**

* Prompting
* Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
* Tools and agents

**Training-Based Improvements**

* Fine-tuning
* Training from scratch

Alternatively, change the heading to *Ways to Improve AI Results* and identify which methods require training.

**Expected Result:** Readers can clearly distinguish between techniques that improve results while using an existing model and techniques that involve changing or creating model parameters through training.

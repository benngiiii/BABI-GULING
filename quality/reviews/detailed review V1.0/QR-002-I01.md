## 3. Review Findings and Suggested Improvements

### Finding 1 — Module Alignment: Machine Learning and Generative AI

**Location:** Chapter 2 and Module Outline
**Priority:** Medium
**Status:** Open

**Finding:**

The Module Outline includes Narrow AI, General AI, Machine Learning, and Generative AI as planned topics for Chapter 2.

However, the current chapter focuses primarily on two classification frameworks:

* **Capability-based classification:** Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI), Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), and Artificial Superintelligence (ASI).
* **Functionality-based classification:** Reactive Machines, Limited-Memory AI, Theory-of-Mind AI, and Self-Aware AI.

Machine Learning and Generative AI are not sufficiently explained in relation to these frameworks.

This may cause confusion because these concepts describe different aspects of AI.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Add a short explanation clarifying that:

* **Machine Learning** is an approach that allows AI systems to learn patterns from data.
* **Generative AI** refers to AI systems that generate content, such as text, images, or audio.
* Neither should be presented as an additional level in the ANI–AGI–ASI classification.

Alternatively, the team may update the Module Outline if the current chapter structure better reflects the agreed learning objectives.

**Expected Result:** The chapter and Module Outline are aligned, and readers can distinguish AI classification frameworks from other AI concepts.

### Finding 2 — Missing Real-World AI Classification Activity

**Location:** Learning Activities and Module Outline
**Priority:** Medium
**Status:** Open

**Finding:**

The Module Outline includes a planned activity where students classify real-world AI applications.

Although Chapter 2 contains ten practice questions, it does not provide a dedicated activity involving several familiar AI systems.

A practical classification activity would help students apply the concepts rather than only remember definitions.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Add a short activity using examples such as:

* Spam detection systems
* Video recommendation systems
* Conversational AI assistants
* Driver-assistance systems

Students should classify each example and briefly explain their reasoning.

Include an answer key with explanations and acknowledge that classifications may depend on a system's actual design and functionality.

**Expected Result:** Students can apply the classification frameworks to familiar AI applications.

### Finding 3 — Visual Comparison of AI Classification Frameworks

**Location:** Sections 2.2–2.4
**Priority:** Low / Optional
**Status:** Optional Improvement

**Finding:**

The existing comparison tables explain the classification frameworks clearly.

However, a simple visual diagram could make their relationships easier for beginners to understand.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Consider adding a diagram that distinguishes:

* Capability-based classification
* Functionality-based classification
* Machine Learning and Generative AI as concepts describing other aspects of AI

The diagram should not suggest that all these concepts belong to one standardized classification hierarchy.

**Expected Result:** Improved visual understanding without introducing misleading relationships.

This improvement is optional because the existing tables already explain the main concepts.

## 3. Review Findings and Suggested Improvements

### Finding 1 — Historical Learning Outcome Consistency

**Location:** Learning Outcomes and Section 1.2
**Priority:** Low
**Status:** Open

**Finding:**

Learning Outcome 2 states that students should be able to describe five key moments in AI history.

However, the historical timeline includes nine milestones, ranging from Alan Turing's 1950 paper to the public release of ChatGPT in 2022.

The additional milestones provide useful context, but the chapter does not identify which five events learners are specifically expected to describe.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Either revise the learning outcome to refer to several important moments in AI history or identify five milestones that students should focus on.

The full timeline can remain as additional learning material.

**Expected Result:** The learning outcome clearly communicates what students are expected to learn.

### Finding 2 — Historical References and Source Verification

**Location:** Sections 1.2 and 1.7
**Priority:** Medium
**Status:** Open

**Finding:**

The chapter includes historical information about the Turing Test, Dartmouth workshop, Arthur Samuel's checkers program, Deep Blue, AlexNet, AlphaGo, Transformers, and ChatGPT.

Relevant academic references are provided, but the historical claims and their supporting sources have not been independently verified.

The distinction between the 1955 Dartmouth proposal and the 1956 workshop is particularly important.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Verify the dates and descriptions against the listed references.

Add brief in-text citations where they would help readers identify the sources of important historical claims.

The existing timeline does not require rewriting if its information is confirmed.

**Expected Result:** Historical information is accurate, traceable, and supported by appropriate references.

### Finding 3 — Incomplete Image Attribution and Licensing

**Location:** Section 1.2, Figures 1.2 and 1.3
**Priority:** Medium
**Status:** Open

**Finding:**

The chapter includes externally sourced images of Alan Turing and a computer similar to Deep Blue.

However, their captions contain unconfirmed licensing information.

The chapter also includes team-created diagrams whose availability at the referenced file paths has not been independently checked.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Verify the original source of each external image and record the correct creator, source, and license information.

Confirm that the team-created diagrams are available at their referenced repository paths.

Replace any external image that cannot be used under the project's requirements.

**Expected Result:** All figures have appropriate attribution and can be displayed in the final study guide.

### Finding 4 — Markdown Rendering and Navigation

**Location:** Table of Contents, figures, tables, and headings
**Priority:** Low
**Status:** Verification Required

**Finding:**

The supplied Markdown uses appropriate headings, tables, image syntax, and internal navigation links.

However, correct source syntax does not guarantee that all images and links will work in the rendered GitHub document.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Preview the chapter in GitHub and confirm that:

* Table of Contents links navigate to the correct sections.
* Images display correctly.
* Tables remain readable.
* Figure captions appear below the correct images.
* Headings and section numbering remain consistent.

**Expected Result:** The chapter displays correctly in its intended viewing environment.

### Finding 5 — Spam-Filter Example and Model Limitations

**Location:** Section 1.5
**Priority:** Low
**Status:** Recommended Improvement

**Finding:**

The spam-filter example clearly demonstrates the relationship between labeled data, training, a model, and inference.

The calculations are consistent with the scoring method, and the chapter explains that the model is simplified.

However, beginners should understand that the scores are derived from only six training emails and do not demonstrate the reliability of a real-world spam detection system.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Preserve the worked example and its correct calculations.

Ensure that the explanation continues to emphasize the limitations of the small dataset and the simplified scoring method.

A short clarification that the scores are not probabilities or direct confidence measurements would further improve technical accuracy.

**Expected Result:** Readers understand both the learning process and the limitations of the example.

### Finding 6 — Terminology and Learning Resource Consistency

**Location:** Learning Outcomes, Section 1.4, and Section 1.7
**Priority:** Low
**Status:** Recommended Improvement

**Finding:**

The chapter introduces important AI terminology and provides a reuse plan for the Study Guide, PPT Slides, and Video Tutorial.

However, the terminology list and explanations should remain consistent across these resources.

The terminology table also includes *Label*, which is not listed among the eight terms in Learning Outcome 4.

**Suggested Improvement:**

Ensure that the learning outcomes accurately reflect the terminology taught in the chapter.

Maintain consistent explanations of data, labels, training, models, and inference across the written material, slides, and video.

**Expected Result:** The chapter and related learning resources use consistent terminology and support the same learning objectives.

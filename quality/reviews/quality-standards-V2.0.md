## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the quality standards and review criteria for the Stage 2 development of the BABI GULING AI Overview project.

Stage 2 focuses on improving and expanding the existing Markdown learning materials and developing the connected learning resources. The purpose of this quality standard is to ensure that all deliverables are accurate, complete, understandable, consistent, and suitable for beginner learners.

The standards apply to the revised Markdown content, Study Guide, Test Bank, PPT Slides, Video Tutorials, and supporting project documentation.

The Markdown content remains the primary source of truth. Other learning resources must be developed based on the approved Markdown content to maintain consistency.

This document is specifically intended for Stage 2 quality reviews and does not replace the Stage 1 quality standards.

---

## 2. Quality Objectives

The Stage 2 quality review aims to ensure that:

1. All eight chapters contain sufficiently complete and developed learning content.
2. Explanations are accurate, clear, logically organized, and suitable for beginners.
3. Important concepts are supported by relevant examples and practical applications.
4. Learning activities include appropriate questions and verified answers or expected outcomes.
5. Chapters are consistent in terminology, explanations, structure, and learning progression.
6. Cross-reviews are conducted by members other than the original content authors.
7. Learner testing is used to identify confusing explanations and improve learning materials.
8. Study Guide, Test Bank, PPT Slides, and Video Tutorials accurately reflect the Markdown source.
9. All important factual claims and references are supported by reliable sources.
10. Project revisions, quality findings, and corrections are properly documented.

## 4. General Quality Assessment Criteria

All Stage 2 deliverables must be evaluated using the following criteria.

### 4.1 Content Accuracy

The content must present correct and reliable information.

Reviewers should check:

* Definitions and explanations are technically correct.
* Important facts and claims are supported by reliable references.
* Examples correctly demonstrate the concepts being explained.
* Formulas, calculations, diagrams, and technical descriptions are accurate.
* No misleading statements or unsupported conclusions are presented.
* Simplified explanations do not change the actual meaning of technical concepts.
* AI-generated information is checked before being included in the final materials.

**Expected result:** The content is factually reliable and does not introduce avoidable conceptual errors.

### 4.2 Content Completeness

Each chapter must cover its agreed learning scope sufficiently.

Reviewers should check:

* All agreed topics and important subtopics are included.
* Important concepts are explained rather than only mentioned.
* Each major topic contains sufficient explanation and relevant examples.
* Important processes are explained in a logical sequence.
* Required diagrams, tables, or other visual explanations are included where useful.
* Learner activities and their answers or expected outcomes are provided.
* No unfinished placeholders or statements indicating that important content will be added later remain.

**Expected result:** Each chapter provides enough information for learners to understand its intended topics without depending entirely on external explanations.

### 4.3 Clarity and Beginner-Friendliness

The materials must be understandable to learners with little or no previous AI knowledge.

Reviewers should check:

* Technical terms are defined when first introduced.
* Explanations use clear and appropriate language.
* Long or complicated ideas are divided into understandable sections.
* Examples help explain unfamiliar concepts.
* Sentences are not unnecessarily complicated.
* Explanations do not assume knowledge that has not been introduced.
* Abbreviations and technical terminology are used consistently.

**Expected result:** A beginner can follow the explanations without frequently needing additional clarification.

### 4.4 Logical Organization and Learning Progression

Content must follow a clear and meaningful learning sequence.

Reviewers should check:

* Topics are arranged in a logical order.
* Sections connect naturally to one another.
* Headings and subheadings accurately describe their content.
* Explanations progress from basic ideas to more advanced ideas.
* Previous concepts are connected to new concepts when appropriate.
* Repeated explanations are minimized unless repetition supports learning.
* The chapter introduction prepares learners for the material that follows.
* The conclusion summarizes important ideas and reinforces learning outcomes.

**Expected result:** Learners can understand how each section connects to the overall chapter.

### 4.5 Examples and Practical Applications

Examples must help learners understand how concepts work in practice.

Reviewers should check:

* Major topics include relevant examples or worked examples.
* Examples are appropriate for the intended beginner audience.
* Each worked example provides enough explanation to follow the reasoning.
* Practical applications demonstrate why the concept is useful.
* Numerical examples and calculations are checked.
* Hypothetical examples are clearly distinguishable from real-world research or results.
* Examples do not introduce unnecessary complexity.

**Expected result:** Learners can connect theoretical explanations with practical situations.

### 4.6 Learning Activities and Answer Verification

Learning activities must provide meaningful opportunities to apply the material.

Reviewers should check:

* Activities are relevant to the section's learning objectives.
* Instructions are clear and understandable.
* Activities test understanding rather than only memorization.
* Questions are answerable using the provided learning materials.
* Suggested answers or expected outcomes are included.
* Answers are checked for accuracy and consistency with the explanations.
* Activities are appropriate for the intended difficulty level.
* Activities encourage learners to explain, compare, classify, calculate, or apply concepts where appropriate.

**Expected result:** Learners can practice the concepts and check whether they understand them.

### 4.7 References and Source Reliability

References must support the factual information presented in the materials.

Reviewers should check:

* Important factual claims are supported by appropriate sources.
* References are relevant to the topic.
* Sources are credible and traceable.
* Author names, titles, publication details, and dates are accurate where available.
* URLs and links work when provided.
* Referenced information is represented fairly and accurately.
* No invented references, publications, quotations, or research findings are included.
* The same source is not used to support a claim that it does not actually establish.

**Expected result:** Readers and reviewers can identify and verify the sources used in the learning materials.

### 4.8 Visual and Diagram Quality

Visual materials must support understanding rather than create unnecessary complexity.

Reviewers should check:

* Diagrams accurately represent the concepts being explained.
* Tables are readable and organized.
* Labels and captions are clear.
* Visual elements are relevant to the surrounding explanations.
* Images and diagrams have appropriate sources or attribution when required.
* Text inside diagrams is readable.
* Visuals do not contradict the written explanations.
* Important processes are presented in a logical visual sequence.

**Expected result:** Visual materials make difficult concepts easier to understand.

### 4.9 Consistency Across Chapters

All eight chapters must work together as one learning module.

Reviewers should check:

* Technical terms are used consistently.
* Definitions do not contradict one another.
* The same concept is not explained in conflicting ways.
* Chapter references and connections are accurate.
* The level of explanation is reasonably consistent.
* Examples and terminology do not create unnecessary confusion between chapters.
* Learning outcomes and chapter content remain aligned.
* Concepts introduced in earlier chapters are used appropriately in later chapters.

**Expected result:** Learners experience the eight chapters as one connected learning module rather than eight unrelated documents.

### 4.10 Formatting and Markdown Quality

Markdown files must be readable, organized, and technically correct.

Reviewers should check:

* Heading levels follow a logical hierarchy.
* Tables render correctly.
* Lists and numbered steps are formatted properly.
* Code blocks use appropriate formatting.
* Links point to the correct files or sources.
* Images and diagrams display correctly.
* File names are clear and consistent.
* Table of contents links work when included.
* No unnecessary formatting errors or broken Markdown elements remain.

**Expected result:** The Markdown content is readable and usable in the GitHub repository and exported documents.

## 10. Cross-Review Standards

Cross-review is required to provide feedback from someone other than the original content author.

### 10.1 Reviewer Independence

* Each core section must be reviewed by a person other than its original author.
* Authors should not be the only people evaluating their own work.
* Reviewers must examine the actual content rather than only its title or outline.
* Review comments must identify specific strengths, weaknesses, or required corrections.

### 10.2 Cross-Review Procedure

1. The author prepares the chapter or section.
2. The content is shared with an assigned reviewer.
3. The reviewer checks accuracy, completeness, clarity, examples, activities, and references.
4. The reviewer records findings and recommendations.
5. The author reviews the feedback and makes necessary revisions.
6. The reviewer checks whether important findings have been addressed.
7. The review result is recorded in the Quality Review Register.

### 10.3 Cross-Review Evidence

The following information should be recorded:

| Information                 | Description                                   |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Review ID                   | Unique identifier for the review.             |
| Reviewed Chapter or Section | The exact material reviewed.                  |
| Original Author             | Member responsible for the content.           |
| Reviewer                    | Member who conducted the review.              |
| Review Date                 | Date of the review.                           |
| Findings                    | Identified strengths, weaknesses, and issues. |
| Recommendations             | Suggested improvements.                       |
| Revision Status             | Whether the findings have been addressed.     |
| Final Result                | Final review decision.                        |

**Expected result:** Every core section receives independent feedback and the review process is traceable.

## 11. Learner Testing Standards

Stage 2 requires learner testing by someone who did not write the section being tested.

The purpose is to determine whether the material can be understood by its intended beginner audience.

### 11.1 Testing Procedure

1. Select a learner or tester who did not write the section.
2. Provide the actual learning material.
3. Ask the tester to read and follow the material independently.
4. Observe whether the tester can understand the explanations and complete the activities.
5. Record the concepts the tester understood.
6. Record confusing explanations, unfamiliar terms, or unclear instructions.
7. Collect suggestions or feedback.
8. Revise the material based on relevant findings.
9. Record the changes and their outcomes.

### 11.2 Required Learner Feedback

The learner-testing record must include:

| Information         | Description                                        |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Tester              | Person who tested the material.                    |
| Tested Section      | Chapter or section being tested.                   |
| Date                | Date of testing.                                   |
| Understood Concepts | Concepts the learner understood correctly.         |
| Confusing Points    | Explanations or terms the learner found difficult. |
| Activity Results    | Whether the learner could complete the activity.   |
| Feedback            | Comments or suggestions from the learner.          |
| Revisions Made      | Changes made based on the feedback.                |
| Follow-Up Result    | Whether the revised material became clearer.       |

### 11.3 Testing Principles

* The tester should not receive explanations from the author before attempting the material.
* Feedback should reflect the tester's actual understanding.
* Confusion should be recorded honestly.
* Revisions should address genuine learning difficulties.
* Testing results must not be invented or exaggerated.

**Expected result:** The learning materials are checked from the perspective of an actual learner rather than only from the perspective of their authors.

## 17. Issue Severity and Review Decisions

Quality findings must be classified according to their impact.

### 17.1 Issue Severity

| Severity   | Meaning                                                                                          | Examples                                                                                                           |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Critical   | A major error that makes the content unreliable, misleading, or unsuitable for use.              | Incorrect fundamental concept, seriously misleading explanation, or major factual error.                           |
| Major      | An important issue that significantly affects learning quality or deliverable completeness.      | Missing required topic, incorrect worked example, missing answers for required activities, or major inconsistency. |
| Minor      | An issue that affects readability or presentation but does not significantly change the meaning. | Typographical errors, small formatting problems, or unclear wording.                                               |
| Suggestion | An optional improvement that could make the material more effective.                             | Additional example, improved diagram, or alternative explanation.                                                  |

### 17.2 Review Decisions

| Decision                      | Meaning                                                                  |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Approved                      | The material meets the required quality standards and can proceed.       |
| Approved with Minor Revisions | The material is acceptable, but small improvements should be made.       |
| Needs Revision                | Important issues must be corrected before approval.                      |
| Not Approved                  | Major or critical problems prevent the material from being accepted.     |
| Pending Review                | The review has not been completed or sufficient evidence is unavailable. |

A review decision must be supported by documented findings.

Critical issues must be corrected before approval. Major issues must be addressed or explicitly resolved before the material is considered ready for final use.

## 18. Review and Revision Procedure

The following procedure is used for Stage 2 quality reviews.

### Step 1 — Submission for Review

The responsible member provides the completed draft and identifies the exact file or version to be reviewed.

### Step 2 — Initial Quality Review

The Quality Lead checks the material against the relevant criteria in this document.

### Step 3 — Findings and Recommendations

The reviewer records:

* Positive findings.
* Identified issues.
* Issue severity.
* Supporting evidence.
* Recommended corrections.

### Step 4 — Author Revision

The responsible author reviews the findings and makes the necessary corrections.

### Step 5 — Recheck

The reviewer checks whether the corrections have addressed the identified issues.

### Step 6 — Final Review Decision

The reviewer records the final result and updates the Review Register.

### Step 7 — Deliverable Consistency Check

If the Markdown source has changed, all affected learning resources must be checked and updated.

### Step 8 — Final Verification

Before Stage 2 submission, the team verifies that all required files are complete, correctly organized, and ready for submission.

## 21. Final Quality Principle

The main objective of Stage 2 quality assurance is not simply to produce longer documents or more learning resources.

The objective is to produce learning materials that are:

* Accurate and reliable.
* Complete and logically organized.
* Understandable to beginners.
* Supported by relevant examples and activities.
* Consistent across all eight chapters.
* Reviewed independently.
* Tested from the learner's perspective.
* Consistent across the Study Guide, Test Bank, PPT Slides, and Video Tutorials.
* Properly documented and traceable.

All team members share responsibility for the quality of the final project.

The Quality Lead coordinates the review process, records findings, checks revisions, and verifies that the required quality standards are met before submission.

## 22. Stage 2 Additions (V2.0 Chapter Template)

These checks apply to every Stage 2 chapter in addition to Sections 4 and 5.

### 22.1 Template and Structure

* [ ] The top of the chapter follows the agreed header: title, **Audience**, **Estimated time**, **Format**, line, Table of Contents, line.
* [ ] The chapter contains Learning Outcomes, Where This Chapter Fits, numbered sections, two missions, Common Misconceptions, Quick Check, Summary and Connections, Answer Key, Glossary and References.
* [ ] The Table of Contents works and matches the headings.

### 22.2 Explanation Style

* [ ] Each new term has a plain one-sentence explanation before the official definition.
* [ ] Sentences are short and the tone is formal but simple.
* [ ] An everyday example comes before the technical example.
* [ ] Hard ideas are broken into steps, with a diagram or a trace table.
* [ ] Every analogy has a "where the analogy breaks" note.

### 22.3 Missions

* [ ] There are two missions, each easy and short (about 10 to 15 minutes).
* [ ] Each mission has Goal, Preparation, Task, Observe, Reflect and Feedback.
* [ ] The reviewer has **recomputed every calculation** and compared it with the Answer Key.
* [ ] The missions are not games and do not depend on a story theme.

### 22.4 Figures and Format

* [ ] Every diagram is an image file stored in the chapter's `assets/` folder; **no Mermaid** or other diagram code is used.
* [ ] Every image path is relative, every image file exists, and every figure has a caption and a source.
* [ ] Figures are readable in light theme and have meaningful text for screen readers (alt text).
* [ ] The chapter uses no GitHub-only features (for example collapsible sections), so it converts cleanly to PDF.
* [ ] Tables are readable: short cells, no more than six columns where possible.

### 22.5 References and Sources

* [ ] The reference list is at the end of the chapter, numbered, with links (or DOI).
* [ ] A person has **opened every link** and recorded the check in the Source Verification table of the Module Outline.
* [ ] Statements about laws, standards or current facts match the cited source.
* [ ] Chapters 4 to 6 are compared with the lecturer's slides, and any missing slide topic is listed.

### 22.6 Test Bank Coverage

Each chapter contains 3 True/False, 3 multiple-choice and 1 short-answer question in its Quick Check. Over eight chapters this gives 24, 24 and 8 questions. The Test Bank must contain at least 20 True/False, 20 multiple-choice and 6 open-ended questions, and each must trace back to a chapter.

### 22.7 Honest Records

* [ ] A review is marked Pass **only** after the reviewer has really read and checked the item.
* [ ] "Pending Review" is used for anything not yet reviewed. No review result, date, name or screenshot is written in advance.
* [ ] AI-use records contain only real uses, with light-theme screenshots where required.
* [ ] Reference projects (Project Plan, section 7) are only those the team has actually opened.

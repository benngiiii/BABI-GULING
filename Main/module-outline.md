## Module Learning Outcomes

After completing this module, learners should be able to:

1. Explain what Artificial Intelligence (AI) is and how it differs from traditional programming.
2. Distinguish AI by capability, including Narrow AI and General AI, and recognize approaches such as Machine Learning and Generative AI.
3. Describe the basic AI workflow, including data collection, algorithms, training, and inference.
4. Identify real-world applications of AI in healthcare, education, transportation, industry, and everyday life.
5. Recognize important AI limitations and ethical concerns, including bias, privacy, transparency, and hallucinations.

Chapters 1 to 5 support learning outcomes 1 to 5 in order.

No prior knowledge of AI or programming is required. Learners need only basic computer and internet literacy.

The chapters follow a fixed order. Chapter 1 introduces the vocabulary used in Chapters 2 and 3, while Chapters 4 and 5 apply these concepts to real-world applications and ethical concerns.

In the documented example, the rule-based filter misses "win money now" and wrongly flags "free lunch at noon", while the learning-based filter classifies both correctly. | Section 1.6 |
| 2 | 2 | Classify familiar AI applications | Classify spam detection, music recommendations, chatbots, driving assistance and image generation | Most current practical applications are Narrow AI. | 3 | 3 | Walk through a basic AI workflow using a diagram | Identify data, algorithms, training, models, inference and evaluation | Data supplies examples. Algorithms define procedures for learning or processing information. Training adjusts a model using data. Inference uses the trained model on new input. Evaluation checks how well the model performs. | Suggested answer: data → training process → trained model → inference → output and evaluation |
| 4 | 4 | Compare AI applications across different fields | Compare healthcare, education, transportation, industry and everyday life, considering uses, benefits and risks | Healthcare: medical-image analysis.
